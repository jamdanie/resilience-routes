# Research and Source Policy

## Purpose

Scenario content should be realistic enough to teach, while remaining transparent about what is factual, simplified, hypothetical, or fictional.

## Source preference

Use sources in this order when practical:

1. Primary government publications and datasets
2. Official standards and technical documentation
3. Peer-reviewed research
4. Recognized professional associations
5. Reputable investigative or explanatory journalism
6. Clearly labeled fictional assumptions created for gameplay

## Required scenario notes

Every scenario proposed for release should document:

- Hazard or disruption type
- Geographic scale
- Infrastructure node affected
- Learning objective
- Sources consulted
- Date sources were checked
- Simplifications made for gameplay
- Whether names, quantities, and timelines are fictional
- Reviewer and approval date

## Avoid

- Unsupported casualty estimates
- Claims that imply certainty where only probability exists
- Sensitive facility details
- Nonpublic vulnerabilities
- Classified, controlled, proprietary, or personally identifying information
- Operational instructions that could facilitate wrongdoing

## In-game disclaimer

The game is an educational simulation and not an operational planning, emergency notification, navigation, cybersecurity, medical, legal, or financial system.
